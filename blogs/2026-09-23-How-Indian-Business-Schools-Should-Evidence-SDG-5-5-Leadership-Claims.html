How Indian Business Schools Should Evidence SDG 5.5 Leadership Claims Without Counting Women’s Day Panels as Governance

Indian business schools and university commerce departments now place the red SDG 5 tile next to a photograph of a 8 March panel. The tile is cheap. Target 5.5 is not. The target is about women’s full and effective participation and equal opportunities for leadership at every level of decision-making. A keynote, a pink backdrop and a cake do not speak to that object until the campus can say who sits on the academic council, who signs the budget, who chairs the placement and IQAC files, and whether pay and promotion move on the same clock for men and women who do the same work.

This briefing is a working method for deans, IQAC coordinators, department chairs and student-affairs officers who have been told to “show gender on the SDG page.” It is written for Indian private B-schools, state university departments and emerging-economy campuses that run large teaching loads, thin research time and a calendar full of commemorative events.

1. The event that is not a leadership claim

A typical Indian gender page reports three artefacts: the share of women in the student body, a list of women guest speakers, and a gallery from International Women’s Day. Sometimes it adds “women in leadership” because a woman is the cultural-committee convenor or because two women sit on a 14-member governing board that meets twice a year. The SDG page then says the campus “advances gender equality in leadership.” That sentence is not always false. It is almost never evidenced.

Target 5.5 is a decision-rights claim. It asks whether women hold positions from which they can change a rule, a budget line, a hiring shortlist or a disciplinary outcome. Visibility is not the same as voice. Voice is not the same as a vote. A vote is not the same as an agenda-setting chair. Campuses collapse those four layers into one photograph.

Working rule. An event becomes an SDG 5.5 claim only when it is tied to a numbered decision body, a defined term, a voting or signing right, and an owner who can produce the attendance and resolution register. A panel is programming.

Three distortions show up every accreditation cycle. First, student gender ratios are used as a proxy for faculty and board leadership. A 48 per cent women BBA cohort says nothing about who chairs the board of studies. Second, “women faculty” is reported as a headcount without rank, without load, and without whether those faculty sit on the committees that allocate research seed money. Third, commemorative activity is booked as impact. SDG 5 cannot survive that accounting.

2. What Target 5.5 actually asks a campus to measure

Do not map the school to “SDG 5.” Open the official list and pick the slice of 5.5 the campus can move with existing offices. For most Indian teaching-first B-schools the honest shortlist is small and uncomfortable.

LayerWhat 5.5 is aboutWhat a campus can report without lying
Governing bodiesEqual opportunity to sit where statutes are changedShare of voting seats on governing board, academic council and finance committee held by women, with term dates
Academic officeWho assigns courses, examiners and research timeShare of chairs, programme heads and board-of-studies convenors who are women, not only “women faculty %”

Target 5.1 (end discrimination) and Target 5.2 (violence) are related but they are not 5.5. A POSH workshop is not a leadership statistic. Target 5.4 (unpaid care) matters on a campus that runs 8 a.m. and Saturday classes without childcare or compressed load for caregiving faculty; name it as 5.4, not as 5.5. Target 5.c (gender-responsive policies) is the policy file: a written promotion clock, a written leave clock, a written rule that committee service is counted in API and not treated as “extra.”

Do not. Print “contributes to SDG 5.5” because the school hosted a women-in-finance conclave. Name the seats, the pay bands and the student offices, or keep the tile off the page.

3. Map who decides, not who appears on stage

Start with the statute, not the brochure. List every body that can bind the school: governing board, finance committee, academic council, board of studies, IQAC, research committee, placement committee, disciplinary committee, hostel committee. For each body record legal name, number of voting seats, number filled, number held by women, chair’s gender, last meeting date, and whether minutes exist. That table is the 5.5 register. It is usually shorter than the events calendar and much harder to decorate.

3.1 Distinguish presence from agenda control

A woman member who receives the agenda the night before and never chairs a subcommittee is present. She is not yet in leadership in the 5.5 sense. Report two numbers: share of seats and share of chairs. If the chairs remain male across five years while seat-share inches up, say so. That pattern is common in Indian private universities where founder-family men retain finance and appointments and women are asked to “own culture and outreach.”

3.2 Do not launder related-party seats

If a woman trustee is a spouse or sibling of the promoter, disclose the related-party flag. Related-party presence can be real influence. It is not evidence of an open leadership pipeline for faculty who do not share a surname. Mixing promoter-family seats into “women on the board” without a flag is a form of SDG-washing that peer teams have started to notice.

Allowed claim

“Of 11 voting seats on the 2025–26 academic council, 3 were held by women; 0 of 4 standing-committee chairs were women; two seats are promoter-family related parties, flagged.”

Disallowed claim

“Women leaders shape every academic decision,” illustrated with a Women’s Day group photograph and no seat table.
A peer team does not need your panel list. It needs to know whether a woman associate professor can put an item on the board-of-studies agenda without asking a male chair for permission the same week.

4. Treat pay, load and promotion as the same file

Leadership that does not show up in pay and time is decorative. Indian commerce departments often run a quiet split: women carry more tutorial sections, more invigilation and more “student welfare,” while research seed money, conference travel and Ph.D. supervision slots concentrate among men who already hold chairs. The API form then punishes the people who kept the programme running.

Rebuild one register with four columns that already exist if anyone joins the HR file to the timetable file: designation, years in rank, fixed cash pay, assigned contact hours, assigned committee hours, research output counted for the last appraisal. Split by gender. Report medians, not only means. Report the promotion clock: months from eligibility to interview, and share of eligible women versus eligible men who were actually interviewed.

Do not hide behind “confidentiality” for aggregates. A campus can publish band-level medians without naming persons. If the school refuses even that, it does not have a 5.5 claim. It has a privacy slogan covering a pay file it does not want read next to the red tile.

Stress test. Hand a visiting examiner ten anonymised faculty rows and the public SDG 5 paragraph. If they cannot see whether women at the same rank work more contact hours for the same or lower fixed pay, the paragraph is brochure copy.

Count acting charge separately. A woman who is “in-charge HOD” for eighteen months without the grade pay, the signing authority or the seat on council is not a 5.5 success. She is a labour subsidy. Report acting charge as acting charge.

5. Count student leadership as office, not as participation

Student gender ratios are a Goal 4 and Goal 5 enrolment story. They are not Target 5.5 unless the school also reports who holds the offices that allocate club budgets, run the student journal, sit on placement committees and speak at the academic council as student nominees. A campus that is 55 per cent women in B.Com and 15 per cent women in the elected executive is not “balanced.” It is a pipeline break.

Build a student-office register the same way as the faculty register: role, selection method (election, nomination, faculty pick), term, gender, and whether the role controls a budget. Faculty-picked “women coordinators” for events are not the same as elected presidents. If the only women officers are cultural and hospitality roles, write that sentence. Do not call it leadership development.

Internship and placement files belong here as well, because 5.5 for students includes whether women receive the same quality of first contract. Median fixed cash pay by gender in the graduating cohort is a 5.5-adjacent number that placement cells already have and rarely print. If the finance-track gap is larger than the operations-track gap, name the track. That is a programme design problem, not a “confidence” sermon.

6. Name the trade-offs with Goals 4, 8 and 10

SDG 5.5 is not a free-standing trophy. A campus that raises women’s enrolment (Goal 4) while keeping women off the board of studies has expanded access without expanding authority. A placement season that raises male median cash pay faster than female median cash pay is a Goal 8 decent-work problem sitting inside a Goal 5 celebration. A leadership pipeline that draws only urban, English-medium, second-generation women is a Goal 10 problem even when the gender seat-share looks respectable.

Write one trade-off paragraph per year. It can be short. “Women hold 27 per cent of voting academic-council seats and 0 of 4 chairs. Female median fixed pay at Assistant Professor is 6 per cent below male median in the same years-in-rank band, and women carry 18 per cent more assigned contact hours. We will not call the year equal leadership until chairs and hours move.” That sentence is more useful to a dean than a new icon.

Do not launder a weak Goal 8 or Goal 10 record by pointing at a rising share of women students. Participation that stops at the classroom door is still a stop. The official language on leadership exists so that campuses cannot hide behind enrolment.

7. Give the claim an owner, a baseline and a stop rule

Every map line needs five parts: unit, target number, local indicator, baseline year, owner. Example: “Faculty and statutory bodies, 2025–26, Target 5.5, share of voting seats and chairs held by women plus median fixed pay by rank and gender, baseline 2023–24 statutory register, Registrar with IQAC co-sign.” If any part is missing, the line stays off the website.

The owner cannot be “the gender champion” or “the women’s cell.” Those offices can run programmes. They cannot certify a board register they do not hold. Name the officer who holds the statute file and the HR officer who holds the pay file. Version the file once after the audit date, not after every conclave. When an acting chair is regularised or a trustee resigns, amend the row; do not leave last year’s percentage on the SDG page.

Add a stop rule. If the school cannot produce a seat-and-chair table and a rank-wise pay median by gender, it will not use the SDG 5.5 tile in the next brochure or NAAC SSR annexure. A stop rule is how you prove the tile is a claim rather than decoration. Without it, the incentive is always to keep the red square, book another 8 March panel, and fix the data later.

Stop rule example. No 5.5 tile if (a) chairs are unreported, (b) acting charge is counted as leadership, or (c) Women’s Day is the only dated activity in the evidence folder.

8. Field checklist and closing brief

Use this list before the IQAC uploads the SDG page or before a dean signs a PRSI or NAAC gender annexure.

  • Numbered target is 5.5 (or 5.4 / 5.c if that is the real claim). The page does not say “SDG 5” as a blob.
  • Seat table exists for every binding body: seats, women seats, chairs, related-party flags, last meeting date.
  • Pay-and-load table exists at designation × gender, with medians and contact hours, not only headcount.
  • Acting charge is labelled as acting charge and is not added to “women HODs.”
  • Student offices are listed by selection method and budget control, not by event attendance.
  • Placement median fixed pay by gender is printed next to the leadership claim or the gap is named.
  • ICC and complaint timelines are in a different paragraph from 5.5, unless the claim is that unsafe campuses destroy participation.
  • Owner, baseline year and stop rule are written in one sentence at the foot of the page.
  • Women’s Day, conclaves and pink posters are in the events annexure. They are not the indicator.

Indian campuses do not lack women who can lead departments, journals, placement files and boards. They lack a habit of counting decision rights the way they already count enrolment. Target 5.5 is the habit. Keep the tile if you will keep the register. Take the tile down if the register is a photograph.

This week’s close-out. Print the statutory-body table. Circle every chair. If the circles are still a single gender, the SDG page is not ready, no matter how strong the 8 March turnout was.
