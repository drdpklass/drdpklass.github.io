How Early-Career Commerce Faculty Should Build a Three-Year API and CAS Plan Before the First Timetable Arrives

Dr. Debasis Pahi | Ph.D. (IIT Kharagpur) | DrDPKlass | drdpklass.com · 11 October 2026

The appointment letter is signed. The joining date is fixed. Then the first timetable arrives and the three-year plan you sketched in the interview evaporates. Early-career commerce faculty in Indian universities and colleges discover, usually in the second semester, that Academic Performance Indicator (API) scores and Career Advancement Scheme (CAS) eligibility are not automatic consequences of “doing good work.” They are the product of a narrow set of countable activities that must be planned, protected, and documented before the administrative calendar consumes the research calendar.

This briefing is for scholars who have just accepted, or are about to accept, an Assistant Professor or equivalent post in commerce, management, or finance. It is not a substitute for the UGC regulations or your university’s CAS statutes. It is a practical sequencing tool so that the activities you choose in year one still produce points in year three, and so that the letter you signed does not quietly make those points impossible.

Why the first three years decide the next ten

UGC regulations on minimum qualifications and CAS have, for more than a decade, treated the first years of an Assistant Professor appointment as the period in which a scholar must accumulate a defined quantum of teaching, research, and academic contribution scores. The exact numbers change with successive regulations, but the structure does not. A scholar who finishes three years with two Scopus-indexed papers, one conference presentation that was actually published in a proceedings volume of recognised quality, and clean teaching feedback will usually clear the next CAS stage with less drama than a scholar who has six book chapters in edited volumes of uncertain standing and a teaching load that never allowed a second draft.

The reason is mechanical. CAS forms ask for dated evidence. Selection committees later ask for the same evidence plus a narrative of contribution. Institutions that face NAAC or NBA cycles in year two will ask every faculty member for the same portfolio at the same moment. If your evidence is not already in folders by that moment, you will spend the month that should have been a revise-and-resubmit writing activity reports instead. The three-year plan is therefore not an aspiration document. It is a calendar that protects the activities that produce points from the activities that only produce workload.

Callout · the silent year-zero

Many new faculty treat the first semester as “settling in.” CAS clocks do not. If your university counts service from the date of joining, the first twelve months are already part of the three-year window. A paper submitted in month ten that is accepted in month sixteen still counts; a paper that is still in outline form in month twelve does not.

What API and CAS actually count in Indian commerce departments

Commerce departments in Indian universities and affiliated colleges operate under two overlapping systems. The first is the UGC’s Academic Performance Indicator framework, which assigns points to teaching, research publications, and academic contributions such as research projects, research guidance, and participation in academic events. The second is the university’s own CAS regulation, which may adopt the UGC numbers, may add local multipliers, or may interpret “research publication” more narrowly than the UGC text.

Three distinctions matter for a new faculty member. First, a paper in a UGC-CARE listed journal is not automatically equivalent to a paper in a Scopus or Web of Science journal for every purpose. Some universities award higher points, or faster CAS movement, for indexed journals. Second, book chapters in edited volumes frequently attract lower points than journal articles, and some universities have begun to discount chapters that appear in volumes without a clear peer-review process. Third, teaching points are usually earned by the fact of teaching a prescribed load, not by innovation. A well-designed active-learning course that takes twice as long to prepare as a standard lecture may earn the same teaching points while consuming research time.

Before you accept the post, obtain the university’s current CAS regulation. Count only the activities that appear in that document. Oral assurances that “we are flexible” do not appear in the CAS form.

Mapping teaching load against research points

The largest threat to a three-year plan is not a lack of ideas. It is the weekly teaching load that is finalised after you join. In many Indian commerce departments an Assistant Professor is assigned four or five papers across undergraduate and postgraduate programmes. When each paper has two or three sections, the clock-hour load can exceed sixteen hours a week before examination duty, NAAC documentation, and student mentoring are added. Research points are earned by papers that require uninterrupted blocks of time. Sixteen contact hours plus preparation and evaluation rarely leave those blocks.

A usable three-year plan therefore begins with a load number. Ask, before the letter is final, what the expected weekly teaching load in clock hours will be in the first two semesters, and whether that number includes or excludes tutorial and practical hours. Ask whether MBA, B.Com., and any weekend or evening batch can be stacked on the same person. If the answer is that “the timetable will be decided later,” treat that as a risk and ask for a written band: for example, not more than twelve contact hours in semester one and not more than fourteen thereafter, with extra load compensated by an honorarium or by a reduction in administrative assignments.

Once the load is known, reverse-engineer the paper calendar. A single empirical paper that uses secondary data available in India can often be drafted in four to six months of protected evenings and one long break. A paper that requires primary data collection, permissions, and fieldwork cannot. If your load will be heavy, the first paper should be the one that can be finished with existing data and existing literature. The second paper can be more ambitious once the first is under review and the administrative calendar is better understood.

Callout · the evaluation load

University examination duty in affiliated colleges is not optional in practice. If you will set, invigilate, and evaluate papers for a large undergraduate programme, those hours must be treated as part of the teaching load when you plan research time. They are rarely written into the appointment letter, yet they reliably appear in October and April.

Building a paper pipeline that survives NAAC season

A three-paper pipeline gives a realistic chance of two advanced papers by the end of year three. Sequence it so one paper is always in a stage that does not require daily attention: (1) secondary-data paper submitted by month eight; (2) extension or second existing source drafted while the first is under review; (3) new-data paper begun only after the first two have left the desk.

NAAC and NBA cycles destroy pipelines when they arrive without warning. Departments typically receive a six-to-twelve-month notice, but individual faculty members often receive a two-week request for “all your publications, conference certificates, and activity reports.” The scholars who survive are those who already maintain a single folder, updated monthly, that contains the PDF of every paper, the acceptance email, the DOI or volume details, and a one-paragraph contribution statement. When the request arrives, the folder is copied, not created.

Journal choice inside the pipeline should be realistic. A desk-rejected paper at a top-tier finance journal is not a failure if it is revised and sent to a solid Scopus-indexed outlet within six weeks. A paper that sits for eight months at a journal whose average review time is longer than the remaining CAS window is a planning error. Early-career faculty should maintain a short list of three journals per paper: one ambitious, one realistic, one safe. The list should be written down before the first submission, not invented after a rejection.

Conference, book chapter, and consultancy traps

Three activities that look productive in the first year frequently fail to produce the points that CAS forms reward. The first is the conference presentation whose certificate is filed and whose proceedings never appear, or appear in a volume that the university later declines to count. Before you pay a registration fee, ask whether the proceedings will be published by a recognised publisher and whether the university has previously awarded points for that series. If the answer is uncertain, treat the conference as professional development, not as a CAS item.

The second trap is the book chapter invitation that arrives with a three-week deadline. Edited volumes can be legitimate, but many invitations that reach new faculty are from publishers whose peer-review process is thin. A chapter that consumes a month of evenings and later receives two or three points, or none, is a poor use of the three-year window. Accept only when the editor is known in your sub-field, the publisher is established, and the deadline allows a proper draft.

The third trap is consultancy or executive-training work that is paid but not documented in a form that CAS recognises. Some universities award academic-contribution points for consultancy; many do not, or require a formal institutional agreement. Before you accept a private training assignment, check whether it must be routed through the university’s consultancy cell and whether the resulting certificate will be accepted. Paid work that does not appear on the CAS form is income; it is not progress toward the next stage.

Documentation habits that make the next CAS form painless

The CAS form is a retrospective document. The work of making it painless is prospective. Three habits are sufficient. First, maintain a single master spreadsheet with columns for activity type, date, evidence file name, points claimed under the current regulation, and status (submitted, accepted, published). Update it on the last working day of every month. Second, store every piece of evidence as a PDF in a dated folder structure: 2026-10-Paper-Acceptance, 2026-11-Conference-Certificate, and so on. Third, write a one-paragraph contribution statement for every paper at the moment of acceptance, while the argument is still clear. Selection committees three years later will not reconstruct the contribution from the abstract alone.

Teaching evidence deserves the same treatment. Save student feedback with semester and course code. Document any curriculum revision or student-project mentoring with a short note. These items rarely carry high points, but they keep the teaching column from looking empty.

Finally, keep a copy of every email that confirms acceptance, funding, or assignment of duty. Institutional email systems are not archives. A personal, backed-up folder is. When the university changes its CAS software or when the department is asked for evidence on short notice, the folder travels with you.

Negotiation points in the appointment letter that protect the plan

The three-year plan is only as strong as the conditions under which it must be executed. Four sentences in the appointment letter, or in a short annex, protect more points than any amount of later effort. First, a named weekly teaching-load band for the first two semesters. Second, a statement that administrative and NAAC assignments will be discussed and will not exceed a reasonable share of non-teaching time. Third, a seed-grant or conference-support amount, even a modest one, with a process for claiming it. Fourth, confirmation that research papers published during the appointment will be treated as the faculty member’s academic work for CAS purposes, with a carve-out for any intellectual-property clause that might otherwise claim them.

These requests are modest. Institutions that intend to support early-career research will usually agree to them in writing. Institutions that will not agree are giving you information about the environment in which the plan must operate. In the latter case the plan should be even more conservative: one paper based on existing data, no new fieldwork in year one, and a personal documentation system that does not rely on institutional systems.

Probation and confirmation language also matter. If confirmation requires a minimum research output, that requirement should be stated, and the output should be achievable inside the load that has been agreed. A letter that is silent on research expectations but later uses the absence of papers as a reason to extend probation is a letter that should have been read more carefully.

Three-year career checklist

Before the first timetable arrives
  • Obtain the current university CAS regulation and identify which publication types carry points.
  • Secure a written teaching-load band for the first two semesters.
  • Choose the first paper: secondary data or existing dataset, target submission by month eight.
  • Create the master spreadsheet and the evidence-folder structure; update monthly.
  • Write the contribution paragraph for the first paper at the moment of acceptance, not later.
  • Decline conference and chapter invitations that do not clearly produce countable points.
  • Confirm that any seed grant or conference support offered in the interview appears in writing.
  • Keep a personal archive of every acceptance email and duty assignment.
  • Revisit the plan at the end of semester one and adjust the second paper’s scope to the actual load.
  • Treat NAAC or NBA documentation requests as copy operations, not as writing projects.

The scholars who clear the next CAS stage with the least friction decided, before the first timetable was printed, which hours would be protected for the activities the form actually counts.
