How Indian Finance Scholars Should Design a Pre-Registration Note for an Archival Study So the Identification Claim Survives Peer Review

Archival finance papers that use Indian data routinely attract a particular kind of reviewer scepticism. The sample looks smaller than the universe of listed firms. Certain years or industries disappear. Robustness tables contain only the specifications that support the headline result. Reviewers, many of whom work with cleaner CRSP–Compustat panels, infer that the author adjusted the sample and the tests after seeing the estimates. In the absence of a written record created before estimation, that inference is difficult to rebut. A concise pre-registration note does not eliminate every data-driven decision that Indian sources force upon the researcher. It does create a dated, inspectable statement of what was locked before the main coefficient was known. That statement changes the conversation from “you must have cherry-picked” to “you documented a deviation and explained it.”

Callout. The note is not a substitute for identification. It is evidence that the identification claim was stated before the data could speak.

1. Why a Pre-Registration Note Changes the Review Conversation in Indian Archival Work

Indian listed-firm panels are not random samples of an underlying population. Firms enter and exit the exchanges. CMIE Prowess and other commercial databases drop or recode observations when identifiers change. SEBI and RBI data releases arrive with lags and revisions. A researcher who begins with the full set of firm-years that satisfy a simple listing criterion will almost always end with a smaller estimation sample. Without a prior written rule, the reduction looks discretionary.

Editors at journals that publish emerging-market finance already know these frictions. What they lack is a transparent record of the rules the author applied before estimation. A pre-registration note supplies that record. It is short—typically two to four pages—and is dated. It can be posted on OSF, a personal website, or simply retained as a time-stamped PDF. When the manuscript is submitted, the note is mentioned in a footnote or online appendix. Reviewers can then compare the locked rules with the reported sample and tests. Deviations remain possible; they are no longer invisible.

The note also disciplines the author. Writing the identification claim and the primary specification before seeing the coefficient reduces the temptation to re-label a robustness check as the main result. In Indian finance, where theory is often applied rather than generated, this discipline is especially useful. The contribution is usually an institutional feature or a data construction that other scholars can replicate. Locking the claim early makes that contribution clearer.

2. What the Note Must Lock Before Estimation

The note is not a full registered report. It is a bounded statement of the decisions that most affect the credibility of an archival identification claim. At minimum it should contain five elements.

First, the research question and the directional hypothesis. State the precise claim that will be tested: for example, “firms that lose a bank relationship after a merger experience a larger decline in short-term debt than matched firms that do not.” Avoid open-ended language such as “we examine the effect of.”

Second, the primary outcome variable and its construction. Define the accounting or market measure, the exact formula, and the source. Note any winsorisation or transformation that will be applied uniformly.

Third, the treatment or shock definition and the control group. Describe how treated firms are identified and how the comparison set is formed. If matching is planned, specify the matching variables and the caliper or bandwidth before any matching is performed.

Fourth, the estimation sample rules. List every inclusion and exclusion criterion that will be applied before the main regression is run. This includes the fiscal-year window, the requirement for consecutive observations, industry filters, and any size or listing-status screens.

Fifth, the primary specification. Write the regression equation, the fixed effects, the clustering level, and the controls that will appear in the baseline table. State that alternative clustering or additional controls will be reported only as robustness.

Callout. If a decision cannot be written before looking at the main coefficient, it does not belong in the locked section of the note. Place it in an exploratory appendix instead.

3. Sample Rules That Survive Listing Changes, Delistings and Database Gaps

Indian data force more sample decisions than most U.S. or European panels. The note should anticipate the most common ones rather than pretend they will not arise.

Begin with the universe. State the source and the initial screen: all firms listed on the BSE or NSE in any year between, say, 2005 and 2023 that report total assets above a stated threshold. Then list the mechanical exclusions that will be applied before any regression: financial firms (if the study is non-financial), firms with missing total assets or sales for two consecutive years, and firms whose primary industry code changes more than once. These rules can be coded and applied without seeing coefficients.

Next, address listing status and delistings. Decide in advance whether the sample will be a balanced panel of continuously listed firms, an unbalanced panel that retains firms until the year of delisting, or a panel that drops the delisting year. Document the choice and the reason. Continuously listed samples reduce survivorship bias in some settings and introduce it in others; the note simply makes the choice visible.

Database gaps require a separate rule. CMIE Prowess sometimes reports a firm under a new company code after a merger or name change. State whether the researcher will manually link codes using the annual report or the exchange website, and whether unmatched codes will be dropped. Also state the treatment of missing observations that appear after a firm has already entered the sample. Dropping every firm with any missing year produces a small, non-representative panel; imputing or carrying forward values introduces measurement error. The note should choose one approach and apply it uniformly.

Finally, record the date on which the sample rules were finalised and the date on which the first regression was run. A short gap is normal; a multi-month gap after which the sample shrinks further will be noticed. The note does not prevent later cleaning, but it forces the author to label any post-estimation cleaning as a deviation.

4. Identification Claims Written Before the Main Coefficient Is Known

The identification section of the manuscript is the part reviewers attack most directly. The pre-registration note protects it by forcing the claim to be written before the result is known.

Write the claim in one paragraph. Identify the source of variation, the institutional feature that generates it, and the assumption that must hold for the coefficient to have a causal interpretation. Example: “The staggered implementation of the RBI’s asset-quality review across banks in 2015–16 provides variation in the intensity of regulatory scrutiny. We compare firms that borrow primarily from banks that received early reviews with firms that borrow primarily from banks that received later reviews. The identifying assumption is that the timing of the review is orthogonal to firm-level demand shocks after controlling for bank fixed effects and firm characteristics.”

Then list the two or three most plausible threats to that assumption and the tests that will be used to address them. Parallel-trends plots, placebo windows, and balance tests on pre-period covariates are standard. State which of these will appear in the main text and which will appear only in the appendix. If a threat cannot be tested with available data, say so. Reviewers are more willing to accept an untestable assumption when it has been acknowledged before estimation than when it appears only after the coefficient is significant.

Indian institutional shocks often arrive with announcement effects or staggered roll-outs that differ by region or bank. The note should specify how the researcher will date the shock—announcement date, effective date, or first observed change in the data—and why that dating is preferred. Changing the dating after seeing the result is one of the easiest ways to move a coefficient across conventional significance thresholds; locking the date removes that degree of freedom.

5. A Robustness Plan That Distinguishes Planned Tests from Exploratory Ones

A robustness table that contains only supportive results is the most common signal of post-hoc adjustment. The pre-registration note reduces that signal by separating planned tests from exploratory ones.

In the note, list the planned robustness checks in advance. Typical items include alternative clustering (firm versus firm-year), winsorisation at 1 percent versus 5 percent, dropping the largest five firms by assets, restricting the sample to NSE-listed firms only, and adding one or two additional controls that reviewers are likely to request. State that these checks will be reported regardless of outcome. Any other specification that is run after seeing the main result will be labelled exploratory and placed in an online appendix with a clear statement that it was not pre-registered.

Also lock the treatment of outliers and influential observations. Decide whether Cook’s distance or DFBETA screens will be applied, and at what threshold. Applying such screens only when they improve the result is transparent once the rule is written; applying them without a prior rule is not.

The note need not anticipate every possible reviewer request. It needs only to show that a core set of alternative specifications was chosen before estimation. When a reviewer asks for an additional test, the author can reply that the test was not pre-registered and is therefore reported as exploratory. That reply is far stronger than silence.

Callout. Exploratory results are not forbidden. They are simply labelled as such. The contribution of the paper rests on the locked tests, not on the exploratory ones.

6. Disclosure Language That Does Not Trigger a Desk Reject

Mentioning a pre-registration note in the manuscript must be done carefully. Some editors still associate the phrase with experimental psychology or with registered reports that constrain the entire paper. The goal is to signal transparency without inviting a desk reject for “not being a conventional archival paper.”

Place the disclosure in a footnote on the first page or in the data section. A workable sentence is: “Sample construction rules, the primary specification, and the list of planned robustness checks were written and dated before estimation began. The note is available in the online appendix.” Avoid the words “pre-registration” or “registered report” in the abstract or introduction if the journal has never published one. The substance—dated rules, locked specification, labelled deviations—matters more than the label.

In the online appendix, include the note itself, the date it was finalised, and a short reconciliation table that maps each locked rule to the corresponding row in the estimation sample or robustness table. Where a deviation occurred, state the reason in one sentence. Common reasons in Indian data include the discovery that a bank merger was not recorded under the original company code, or that an RBI circular was later amended. These explanations are credible when they are short and factual.

If the journal’s submission system allows supplementary files, upload the note as a PDF with a clear filename. Do not bury it inside a 40-page appendix. Reviewers who wish to inspect it should be able to find it in under a minute.

7. Pre-Empting the Most Common Reviewer Objections

Three objections appear repeatedly in reports on Indian archival papers. The note can be written so that each is answered before it is raised.

The first is that the sample is selected. The reply is the locked list of mechanical exclusions and the reconciliation table. If the estimation sample is 40 percent of the initial universe, the table shows exactly which rules produced the reduction and confirms that those rules were applied before any regression.

The second is that the identification claim is post-hoc. The reply is the paragraph in the note that states the claim, the institutional feature, and the identifying assumption, dated before the main coefficient was estimated. Parallel-trends figures and placebo tests that were listed in advance further support the claim.

The third is that only favourable robustness checks are shown. The reply is the list of planned checks and the statement that any additional specification is labelled exploratory. When a reviewer requests a new test, the author can add it as exploratory and note that it was not part of the original plan.

None of these replies guarantees acceptance. They do change the burden of proof. The reviewer must now argue that the locked rules themselves are unreasonable, rather than that the author adjusted the rules after seeing the result. That is a harder argument to sustain when the rules are written clearly and applied consistently.

8. Field Checklist for Indian Finance Scholars

  • Write the research question and directional hypothesis in one sentence before any estimation.
  • Define the primary outcome, treatment, and control group with exact construction rules.
  • List every mechanical sample inclusion and exclusion criterion, including treatment of delistings and missing codes.
  • Write the primary regression equation, fixed effects, clustering, and baseline controls.
  • List the planned robustness checks that will be reported regardless of outcome.
  • Date the note and retain a time-stamped copy before running the first main regression.
  • Prepare a one-page reconciliation table that maps locked rules to the reported sample and tables.
  • Disclose the existence of the note in a footnote or online appendix using neutral language.
  • Label any post-estimation change as a deviation and explain it in one factual sentence.
  • Place exploratory specifications in a clearly marked appendix section.

A pre-registration note will not convert a weak identification strategy into a strong one, nor will it compensate for a poorly constructed variable. What it will do is remove the most common ground for reviewer scepticism in Indian archival finance: the suspicion that sample and specification choices were made after the coefficients were known. For scholars who work with listing changes, database gaps, and staggered institutional reforms, that removal is often the difference between a revise-and-resubmit and a rejection that cites “concerns about sample selection.” The note is short to write, inexpensive to store, and disproportionately useful at the moment the referee report arrives.
