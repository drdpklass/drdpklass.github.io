How Indian Scholars Should Read a Ministry of Finance Notification on Green Bond Tax Incentives Before Estimating the Greenium

A Ministry of Finance notification that grants a tax incentive on green bonds is not an exogenous shock that can be dropped into a difference-in-differences regression. It is a negotiated, eligibility-gated, and frequently stacked fiscal measure whose text must be read before any greenium is estimated. Scholars who treat the date of the gazette as the treatment date, ignore the definition of “green,” or fail to track concurrent SEBI or RBI measures will produce coefficients that cannot be defended. This briefing shows how to read such a notification the way a tax counsel and a fixed-income desk would read it, then how to translate that reading into a defensible empirical design for the Indian market.

Why the Notification Is Not a Clean Instrument

Green bonds in India already trade in a thin market dominated by public-sector issuers, infrastructure funds and a small set of private corporates that can meet SEBI’s BRSR Core thresholds. A tax incentive—whether a withholding-tax exemption for foreign portfolio investors, a deduction under Section 80EEA-style language, or a reduced coupon tax for domestic mutual funds—changes the after-tax yield for only a subset of investors and only for bonds that satisfy the notified definition of green. The same notification usually contains a sunset clause, a minimum tenor, a use-of-proceeds reporting obligation, and an explicit statement that the incentive cannot be claimed if another specified benefit is already being taken. These features are not footnotes; they are the treatment itself. In the Indian institutional setting the Ministry of Finance does not announce a pure price subsidy; it designs a targeted fiscal instrument that must survive the scrutiny of the Comptroller and Auditor General, the Revenue Department, and the securities regulator. Consequently the legal text is dense with conditions that an empirical researcher must code before any regression is run.

Callout. The greenium is the residual price difference after all observable risk and liquidity factors are controlled. If the tax incentive is available only to certain investors or only for certain tenors, the residual will partly reflect tax clientele effects rather than a pure preference for green projects. The notification tells you which clienteles are affected.

What the Ministry Actually Notifies

Start with the gazette number, the date of issue, and the exact statutory provision being amended or newly inserted. Ministry of Finance notifications on capital-market incentives are usually issued under the Income-tax Act or under the Finance Act of the relevant year. They rarely create a brand-new tax head; they modify an existing schedule, insert a proviso, or prescribe a form. Scholars should extract four pieces of information before any data work begins:

  • The precise definition of “green bond” or “eligible bond” that the notification adopts. It may cross-refer to a SEBI circular, an RBI master direction, or an external taxonomy. That cross-reference is binding.
  • The class of investors who can claim the incentive (resident, non-resident, FPI, mutual fund, insurance company, etc.).
  • The maximum tenor, minimum issue size, or listing requirement that must be met.
  • The effective date and the sunset date, including any transitional provision for bonds issued before the notification but still outstanding.

If the notification is silent on any of these four points, the silence itself is information: the incentive may be narrower than the press release suggested. Press notes issued by the Department of Economic Affairs or the Central Board of Direct Taxes are useful for context but are not the law. Only the gazette text controls. Scholars should also note the language of the accompanying explanatory memorandum that sometimes appears in the Finance Bill. While the memorandum does not override the statute, it frequently clarifies the government’s intended scope and can be cited in a paper as evidence of legislative purpose when the statutory text is ambiguous. In practice, many Indian tax notifications on capital-market instruments are issued as subordinate legislation under an enabling provision of the Income-tax Act; the enabling section must be identified so that the hierarchical relationship between the Act, the Rules and the notification is clear to the reader.

Eligibility, Sunset and Stacking Rules

Eligibility is the first filter. Many Indian green-bond incentives require the issuer to maintain a separate escrow or to furnish a use-of-proceeds certificate within a fixed number of days after each coupon date. Failure to file the certificate can retroactively disqualify the bond. For empirical work this means that the set of “treated” bonds is not fixed on the issue date; it can shrink later. Scholars should therefore collect the list of bonds that actually received the tax benefit, not merely the list of bonds labelled green by the exchange.

Sunset clauses create a natural end to the treatment. If the incentive expires on a known date, bonds issued after that date become a clean control group only if no successor scheme is announced in the same Finance Act. Stacking rules are equally important. The notification frequently states that the benefit shall not be available if the investor has already claimed a deduction under another section or if the bond is already exempt under a tax treaty. Ignoring stacking produces an overstated greenium because part of the price difference is simply the avoidance of double taxation.

Callout. Always check whether the same Finance Act also amended the withholding-tax rates on ordinary corporate bonds. A simultaneous reduction in the ordinary rate compresses the relative advantage of the green incentive and can reverse the sign of the estimated greenium.

Mapping the Incentive to the Pricing Equation

Once the legal text is clear, map it onto the bond pricing identity. The after-tax yield for an investor who can claim the incentive is:

\[ y_{AT} = y_{BT} \times (1 - t_i) + \text{other adjustments} \]

where \( t_i \) is the effective tax rate reduction granted by the notification to investor class \( i \). The greenium is then the difference in pre-tax yields between otherwise identical green and brown bonds after controlling for credit risk, duration, liquidity and the investor tax clientele. If only FPIs receive the incentive, the relevant comparison is the yield demanded by FPIs on green versus brown bonds of the same issuer, not the average market yield. Indian secondary-market data rarely identify the investor type of each trade, so scholars must either use primary-market allotment data (where investor categories are reported) or construct a proxy based on the share of FPI holdings disclosed in the quarterly shareholding pattern of the issuer’s listed bonds.

For domestic mutual funds the mapping is different. A deduction or exemption at the fund level flows through to the NAV and therefore affects the secondary-market price only to the extent that the fund is a marginal buyer. The notification’s language on whether the benefit is available at the fund or at the unit-holder level determines whether the price impact appears in the bond market or in the mutual-fund flow data. In the Indian context, several large mutual-fund houses also manage separate debt schemes that are open to both retail and institutional investors; any differential tax treatment can therefore generate cross-scheme arbitrage that further complicates the observed yield. Scholars should document the relevant scheme categories and, where possible, obtain scheme-level holding data from the Association of Mutual Funds in India monthly disclosures. Failure to do so leaves an open channel through which the tax incentive can affect prices without appearing in the investor-category weights constructed from primary-market allotments alone.

Identification Problems Unique to the Indian Market

Three identification problems dominate Indian green-bond studies. First, almost all large green issuances are by public-sector entities whose credit risk is quasi-sovereign. A simple matched-pair design that compares a green PSU bond with a brown PSU bond of the same issuer still leaves the possibility that the green bond was issued under a government mandate that also improved other terms (longer tenor, larger size, or an explicit government guarantee). The notification itself sometimes contains such a guarantee or a priority in the government’s borrowing calendar; that language must be coded as a separate control.

Second, SEBI’s listing and disclosure requirements for green bonds changed several times between 2017 and 2025. Any study that spans more than one regulatory regime must interact the tax-incentive dummy with a SEBI-regime dummy. Otherwise the coefficient on the tax incentive absorbs the effect of tighter disclosure. Third, the Indian yield curve is estimated from a small number of liquid benchmark securities. Illiquid green bonds will show large bid-ask spreads; failure to control for the Amihud measure or for the number of trades per month will attribute the liquidity premium to the green label. A fourth, less discussed problem is the interaction with the Priority Sector Lending framework and with the Reserve Bank’s climate-risk guidance to banks. When a bank purchases a green bond that also qualifies as a priority-sector asset or that reduces its reported climate-risk exposure, the bank’s demand curve shifts for reasons unrelated to the tax incentive. Coding these overlapping regulatory benefits requires a careful reading of the relevant RBI circulars that were in force on the issue date of each bond.

Callout. If the notification requires the bond to be listed on a recognised stock exchange, unlisted green loans or privately placed bonds are not in the treatment group. Including them produces attenuation bias.

Data Sources and Practical Cleaning Steps

Useful public sources include the Ministry of Finance gazette repository, the SEBI website for green-bond listings, the RBI’s database on corporate bond issuances, and the monthly FPI investment data published by NSDL. For secondary-market prices, NSE and BSE trade-level files (available to registered researchers) or the CCIL’s reported yields can be used. The practical cleaning sequence is:

  1. Download the exact gazette PDF and extract the operative paragraphs into a short legal memo.
  2. Build an issuer-bond panel that flags every bond that meets the notification’s definition on the relevant dates.
  3. Merge primary-market allotment data to recover investor-category weights.
  4. Compute duration-matched yield spreads against the government securities curve and against the issuer’s own brown bonds.
  5. Winsorise or drop bonds with fewer than five trades in a month before estimating the greenium regression.

Scholars should also collect the use-of-proceeds reports that issuers file. Bonds that later fail the reporting test should be reclassified as untreated after the failure date; treating them as permanently treated inflates the estimated effect.

What Reviewers Will Ask

A careful reviewer will ask four questions. First, did the authors read the gazette or only the press release? Second, is the treatment dummy time-varying and does it respect the sunset and disqualification rules? Third, have concurrent changes in ordinary bond taxation, SEBI green-bond regulations, and RBI liquidity measures been controlled? Fourth, is the greenium estimated within investor clienteles that actually receive the tax benefit? Papers that cannot answer these questions are usually desk-rejected or sent back for a major revision. The safest design is a triple-difference: green versus brown, pre versus post notification, and eligible-investor-heavy versus eligible-investor-light issuers, with the eligibility defined strictly by the gazette text.

Closing Checklist for Scholars

  • Gazette number, date and exact statutory provision extracted and quoted in the paper’s appendix.
  • Definition of “green” cross-checked against the referenced SEBI or RBI document.
  • Investor classes eligible for the incentive listed and matched to available holding data.
  • Sunset date, stacking rules and reporting obligations coded as time-varying indicators.
  • Concurrent Finance Act changes to ordinary bond taxation documented.
  • Primary-market allotment or FPI holding data used to construct clientele weights.
  • Liquidity and credit controls included; public-sector quasi-guarantee status flagged.
  • Use-of-proceeds compliance checked; non-compliant bonds reclassified.
  • Triple-difference or equivalent design presented as the main specification.
  • Robustness table shows results when the treatment window is shortened to the period before any successor scheme.

Further reading on DrDPKlass: earlier Policy Analysis notes on reading RBI circulars and SEBI LODR amendments supply the complementary regulatory-reading protocol that should sit beside this tax-notification checklist.

Reading the notification before estimating the greenium is not extra work; it is the identification strategy. Scholars who treat the gazette as a clean instrument will continue to report greeniums that disappear once the tax clientele, the stacking rules and the concurrent regulatory changes are properly measured. The Indian market is small enough that these details are observable; they should therefore be measured.
