A robustness section that arrives as an afterthought is one of the fastest ways an otherwise clean Indian finance paper is returned by an associate editor. Reviewers at journals that still desk-reject heavily treat the robustness suite as a signal of whether the author has already stress-tested the claim against the objections that any careful reader of Indian corporate or bank data will raise. When the section merely re-runs the baseline with an extra control or a different winsorisation cut-off, the paper signals that the author has not yet done the work of anticipating the three attacks that almost always appear. This briefing gives a practical sequence that early-career scholars can complete in a weekend once the main specification is stable, so that the robustness suite becomes evidence rather than decoration.

Why Most Robustness Sections in Indian Finance Papers Fail the Associate Editor Screen

In Indian corporate finance submissions the most common robustness pattern is a short table that adds firm fixed effects, drops the financial-crisis years, and winsorises at 1 percent instead of 5 percent. Each of these checks is reasonable; none of them addresses the objection the reviewer is most likely to write. The associate editor sees a paper whose identification rests on a regulatory change or a state-level policy, yet the robustness section never shows what happens when the treatment is measured with a one-year lag, when firms that lobby are dropped, or when the parallel-trends assumption is tested with leads. The result is a desk reject that cites “insufficient robustness” even though the paper contains four extra tables.

The underlying problem is sequential rather than technical. Authors finish the main result, then invent checks that are easy to run. Reviewers start from the opposite end: they read the institutional setting, list the three ways the claim could be an artefact of Indian data structures, and look for evidence that the author has already ruled those ways out. When the match is poor, the paper is treated as unfinished. A robustness suite that anticipates the attacks converts the same empirical work into a signal of maturity.

Callout. Count the number of robustness tables that would still be informative if the reviewer had never seen the main result. If the answer is fewer than three, the suite is still decorative.

Map the Three Most Likely Reviewer Attacks Before You Write a Single Check

Before any additional regression is estimated, write a one-page memo that names the three most plausible attacks a reviewer who knows Indian data will raise. The exercise is cheap and forces the author to confront the paper’s weakest points while the main specification is still malleable. For a typical firm-level study that uses the Companies Act or a SEBI regulation as a shock, the three attacks are almost always some combination of the following.

  • Measurement of the treatment or outcome is contaminated by concurrent reforms. Many Indian regulatory changes arrive in clusters. A paper that treats the 2013 Companies Act as a clean shock to board independence must show that results survive when firms affected by the simultaneous changes in related-party transaction rules or CSR mandates are excluded.
  • The parallel-trends or exclusion restriction fails because of pre-existing trends in promoter-controlled firms. Indian ownership is concentrated. Reviewers routinely ask whether the treated firms were already on a different trajectory because of family succession, group support, or differential access to bank credit.
  • Sample composition or attrition is non-random. Firms that delist, merge, or stop filing under MCA after the reform are not missing at random. A robustness suite that ignores the firms that exit the CMIE or Prowess panel invites the charge that the result is an artefact of survivors.

The memo should also record, in one sentence each, the exact test that would falsify the attack. Once those three tests are written, the rest of the suite is mechanical. Scholars who skip this step almost always produce robustness tables that the reviewer can dismiss in a single paragraph.

Build the Core Robustness Suite: Functional Form, Sample, and Measurement

With the three attacks named, construct a core suite of six to eight specifications that any associate editor can scan in under ten minutes. The suite has three layers.

Functional form and fixed effects

Report the main result under firm and year fixed effects, under firm and industry-year fixed effects, and under a first-difference specification if the outcome is a stock variable. Indian papers that rely only on firm fixed effects are frequently asked whether industry-wide policy responses are absorbed. Adding the industry-year layer answers the question without requiring a new identification argument. If the coefficient remains stable within 15–20 percent of the baseline, state that fact in the text rather than leaving the reader to compute it.

Sample restrictions that map to Indian institutional features

Always show the result after dropping government-owned firms, after dropping firms that belong to large business groups (as classified by CMIE), and after restricting to firms that appear in every year of the panel. These three cuts address the most common composition critiques. In addition, for papers that use a regulatory threshold (for example, the ₹1,000 crore net-worth cut-off for CSR or independent-director requirements), report the result on a narrow bandwidth around the threshold and on the full sample. The bandwidth result shows that the finding is not driven by the largest or smallest firms.

Alternative measurement

Replace the preferred measure of the outcome with the most common alternative used in the Indian literature. If the paper studies investment, show both capital expenditure scaled by assets and the change in net fixed assets. If it studies leverage, show both book and market leverage. Document the correlation between the two measures in a footnote so the reviewer can see that the choice is not driving the result. For treatment variables based on regulatory status, show the result when treatment is coded as a continuous intensity (for example, the fraction of the board that must be independent) rather than a binary indicator.

Practical rule. Every robustness column should be accompanied by a one-sentence statement of what objection it addresses. Tables that lack this sentence are treated as fishing.

Address Identification Threats That Indian Corporate Data Invites

The core suite handles functional form and composition. Identification threats require a second layer of tests that are specific to the research design. For difference-in-differences designs that exploit staggered Indian state or regulatory changes, report three diagnostics.

  1. An event-study plot with at least three leads and three lags, with confidence intervals. Annotate the plot with the exact regulatory dates so the reviewer can see that no anticipatory movement occurs before the legal effective date.
  2. A test that drops the always-treated or never-treated groups if the design is staggered. Recent econometric work has shown that the two-way fixed-effects estimator can be biased when treatment timing varies; Indian papers that ignore this are increasingly flagged.
  3. A placebo test that assigns treatment on a date one or two years earlier and shows a null result. The placebo should use the same sample and fixed effects as the main specification.

For instrumental-variables designs the suite must include the first-stage F-statistic, the reduced-form result, and a test of the exclusion restriction that uses an alternative instrument or a placebo instrument that should be correlated with the endogenous variable but not with the outcome. Indian papers that instrument with a lagged regulatory variable often face the objection that the lag itself is correlated with unobserved firm quality; the suite should therefore show the result when the instrument is lagged an additional year and when firms that lobbied in the preceding period are excluded.

In both designs, include one specification that interacts the treatment with a pre-reform measure of the firm’s exposure (for example, the pre-period share of related-party transactions or the pre-period bank dependence). If the interaction is insignificant, the paper has evidence against the most common omitted-variable story. If it is significant, the interaction itself becomes a substantive finding that can be discussed rather than hidden.

Handle Multiple Testing and Selective Reporting Without Losing the Main Result

Indian finance papers frequently examine several outcomes or several cuts of the same outcome. Reviewers increasingly ask whether the reported significance survives a correction for multiple testing. The practical response is not to abandon the main result but to be transparent about the family of tests.

First, pre-specify the primary outcome and the primary sample in the identification section. All other outcomes are labelled secondary. Second, report the number of specifications estimated in the course of the project (including those not shown) in a short footnote. Third, for the family of secondary outcomes, apply a simple Bonferroni or Romano–Wolf step-down adjustment and report both the unadjusted and adjusted p-values. In most cases the primary result remains significant after adjustment; the secondary results that do not survive are then correctly described as suggestive.

Selective reporting is addressed by archiving the full set of robustness do-files or R scripts with the replication package. The earlier DrDPKlass note on building a replication package that reviewers will actually open explains the minimum documentation required. When the scripts are available, the reviewer can verify that the author did not simply stop once a significant coefficient appeared. The existence of the archive is itself a robustness check.

Callout. If the paper examines more than three outcomes, the primary outcome must be named in the abstract. Reviewers treat papers that rotate through outcomes until one is significant as exploratory, regardless of the quality of the data.

Document and Present the Suite So Reviewers Can Verify in Ten Minutes

A robustness suite that cannot be verified quickly is treated as incomplete. The presentation should follow a fixed order that mirrors the three attacks named in the memo.

  • Place a summary table of the six to eight core specifications immediately after the main result table. Use a common sample and common fixed effects so that coefficient changes are comparable. Bold the baseline column.
  • Follow with the event-study or first-stage figure. Keep the figure to a single panel; multi-panel figures that require the reader to hunt for the relevant coefficient are ignored.
  • Relegate the remaining checks (alternative clustering, different winsorisation, exclusion of particular states) to an online appendix and state in the text that the results are available there. The appendix should open with a one-page roadmap that maps each table to the attack it addresses.
  • In the text, write a short paragraph that begins “The three most plausible alternative explanations are…” and then reports, in one sentence each, the result of the corresponding test. This paragraph is what the associate editor reads; the tables are what the reviewer consults only if the paragraph is unconvincing.

Finally, add a single sentence in the data section that records the exact date on which the CMIE, MCA, or RBI data extract was downloaded and the version of any classification file used. Indian data vendors update firm identifiers and group affiliations; a robustness check that cannot be replicated because the underlying extract has changed is useless. The sentence costs nothing and removes one common referee complaint.

Field Checklist for the Next Submission

Before the robustness section is written

  • One-page memo naming the three most likely reviewer attacks and the exact test that would falsify each.
  • Primary outcome and primary sample named in the identification section.

Core suite completed

  • Firm + year and firm + industry-year fixed effects shown.
  • Government firms, business-group firms, and balanced-panel restriction shown.
  • Alternative measurement of outcome and, where relevant, of treatment shown.
  • Event-study or first-stage diagnostics included for the chosen design.
  • Multiple-testing adjustment reported for any secondary outcomes.

Presentation ready

  • Summary table of core specifications placed immediately after the main result.
  • Text paragraph that maps each attack to its test result.
  • Online appendix roadmap and full scripts archived with the replication package.
  • Data-extract date and version recorded in the data section.

A robustness suite built this way does not guarantee acceptance, but it removes the most common reason an Indian finance paper is returned before it reaches a full review. The work is front-loaded: once the three attacks are named and the core tests are coded, every subsequent paper in the same empirical setting can reuse the same template. Early-career scholars who treat the suite as part of the identification argument rather than as a post-script produce papers that reviewers recognise as finished.
