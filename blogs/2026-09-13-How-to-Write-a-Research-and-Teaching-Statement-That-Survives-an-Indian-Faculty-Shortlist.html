How to Write a Research-and-Teaching Statement That Survives an Indian Faculty Shortlist

Indian faculty shortlists are not US job-market packets with a search chair who reads every sentence. They are files that a dean, a subject expert, an IQAC nominee and sometimes a visitor from another university will open between two other interviews. Your research-and-teaching statement is not a memoir. It is a scoring document. If a reader cannot extract, in four minutes, what you will publish next, which papers you can teach next semester, and how you will not collapse the department’s API or NAAC evidence file, the statement has failed—even if it is eloquent.

This briefing is written for early-career candidates in commerce, finance, economics and management who are applying to central universities, state universities, IIMs and private universities that still run UGC-style selection. The same discipline helps candidates in Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and parts of Africa where public universities score teaching load and publication lists more tightly than “intellectual trajectory.” It is not a template for a North American research university that wants a five-page theory of your mind.

The working unit is two to three pages. Longer files are not read. Shorter files look empty. What follows is how to fill those pages so a committee can defend your name when the vice-chancellor asks why you, not the candidate with one extra Scopus paper.

1. Write for the committee that will sit, not for a US search chair

A typical Indian selection committee is mixed by statute. One member will care about journal lists and citations. One will care whether you can take the vacant Financial Management section in July. One will glance at whether your file will survive an NAAC peer-team question about student-centric methods. One may only check whether your Ph.D. awarding institute and your category certificate are in order. Your statement must give each of them a sentence they can underline.

That is why imported templates fail. A US-style research statement that opens with “I am fascinated by the intersection of…” wastes the first screen. A teaching philosophy that opens with Freire or “I believe every student can learn” wastes the second. Indian committees are not hostile to philosophy. They are hostile to un-scorable prose. Give them nouns: journals, courses, batch sizes, methods, and the next two working papers with target outlets.

Do not. Paste a statement you wrote for an overseas postdoc and change the letterhead. The committee will hear the wrong audience in the first paragraph—and they will assume you have not read the advertisement.

Read the advertisement twice before you draft. Circle the exact phrases: “ability to teach at undergraduate and postgraduate levels,” “evidence of research in ABDC / Scopus,” “experience with NAAC / NBA documentation,” “willingness to take administrative responsibility.” Those phrases are the rubric. Echo them with evidence, not with synonyms.

2. Build a research statement that names a pipeline, not a dream

Committees do not hire a topic. They hire a person who will still be submitting papers in year three when the first CAS file is due. Structure the research half in four blocks, each short.

2.1 One sentence of identity

Name the field, the method family, and the India (or emerging-economy) site in one sentence. Example: “I study how disclosure and credit-market frictions shape firm behaviour in India, using panel methods and, where the design allows, policy variation.” That sentence is your label. Everything after it must be an instance of that label. If your job-market paper is on ESG ratings and your second paper is on classroom clickers, you do not have a statement. You have two CVs stapled together. Either drop the orphan paper from the statement or write the honest connective tissue—if there is none, leave the orphan on the CV only.

2.2 What is already in print or under review

List at most three pieces. For each: question, design in one clause, status (published / R&R / under review), outlet or target. Do not narrate the literature review. The committee has your papers or will request them. What they need is proof that you can finish.

2.3 The next 24 months

Name two working papers with a sentence each on data access and target journal band. “I will also work on climate risk” is not a pipeline. “Paper B uses CMIE Prowess and BRSR filings for 2019–25; first draft December; target Journal of Banking & Finance / JCF-equivalent band” is a pipeline. If data access depends on a co-author or a paid database the new campus may not have, say so and name a fallback sample. Committees have been burned by candidates whose entire agenda required a Bloomberg terminal the college will never buy.

2.4 How the campus is served

One short paragraph on doctoral mentoring, seminar culture, or a lab you can run with existing software. Do not promise a centre of excellence. Promise a fortnightly reading group and a methods clinic for M.Com. dissertations. Those are things a head of department can picture.

Working rule. If a member can write “two papers in flight, data in hand, can join the finance seminar” in the margin, the research half has done its job.

3. Write teaching as load, method and evidence

Indian departments hire teaching first, even when the advertisement is dressed as research. The teaching half of the statement should answer three questions a timetable clerk would ask.

QuestionWhat to put on the page
What can you teach in July?A list of courses you have already taught or TAed, plus two adjacent courses you can prepare in six weeks. Match names to the department’s current handbook, not to your Ph.D. transcript.
How do you run a 60-student section?One method you actually use: case + cold-call, problem sets with a marking rubric, short writing with a feedback budget. Name the batch size you have handled.
What evidence exists?Scores if they are clean; a sample assignment; a before-after on one skill. Do not paste a full student-comment dump.

First-generation classrooms are the default in much of Indian public higher education. If you have taught such rooms, say how you sequence language and worked examples. If you have only taught elite master’s students, do not pretend otherwise; say how you will adapt office hours and reading load. Committees can smell a philosophy imported from a MOOC.

Do. Attach, or offer to send, one annotated syllabus of two pages: week plan, assessment weights, and the one writing or problem task that carries the course. A syllabus is more persuasive than a paragraph on “learner-centric pedagogy.”

If the campus is under NAAC or NBA, add four lines on how your course produces artefacts: rubrics, sample scripts, CO-PO map. You are not applying to be IQAC coordinator. You are showing that your file will not create extra work for the person who is.

4. Map the department’s actual courses and the institute’s API maths

Fit is not flattery. Fit is a table in your head that never needs to appear as a table on the page.

Open the department website and the last available syllabus booklet. Write the vacant or overloaded courses in a notebook. In the statement, name two of them explicitly: “I can take Corporate Finance at the MBA level and Research Methodology for the M.Com. dissertation batch.” Generic willingness (“I am happy to teach across the curriculum”) reads as having read nothing.

Then do the API arithmetic the committee will do. UGC Career Advancement still shapes how public universities think, even where the latest gazette has moved the furniture. A candidate who will add Scopus papers, a funded project attempt, and a usable teaching file is easier to promote than a candidate who will only add committee work. Your statement should make the promotion path obvious without saying “I want CAS.” Name the next two outputs that will sit in Category III. Name one realistic sponsored-project call (ICSSR, UGC, institute seed grant) you would apply to in year one. Do not invent a DST-level lab.

Public university reader

Looks for teachable load, category certificates, and whether your papers will score. Keep journal names and ISSN-ready citations in the CV; keep the statement free of citation stuffing.

IIM / research-leaning private

Looks for pipeline and seminar presence. Still wants two courses you can take without a year of prep. Do not hide teaching; compress it.

If the advertisement is for a specific sub-field—accounting, marketing analytics, public policy—do not lead with your secondary interest because it is trendier. The visitor subject expert was invited for the sub-field on the file. Surprise them and you lose the expert vote.

5. When they ask for one file, keep two voices with one spine

Many Indian portals still want a single “research and teaching statement” upload. Write it as two labelled sections with a three-line bridge at the top.

The bridge names the spine that joins the two halves. Good spines are concrete: “My research on disclosure quality is the same muscle I use when I teach students to read an annual report instead of a summary slide.” Bad spines are slogans: “My teaching informs my research and my research informs my teaching.” Committees have read that sentence two hundred times this year.

Keep research first if the post is Assistant Professor at a research-leaning school. Keep teaching first if the post is in a college where the workload circular is already 16 hours. Order is a signal. Do not hide the weaker half at the back; shorten it and make it specific.

One spine, two labelled sections, no third meditation on “my journey.” The journey belongs in the interview if someone asks. It does not belong on page one of a file that ten people will skim.

File hygiene matters more than candidates think. PDF, searchable text, your name in the header, page numbers, a filename that matches the portal’s logic: Surname_ResearchTeaching_Institute.pdf. Image-only scans from a phone fail when the expert prints in black and white.

6. Rehearse the five-minute oral version before you attach the PDF

The statement is also a script. In most Indian interviews you will be asked, early, “Tell us about your research” and “What can you teach?” If your spoken answer wanders from the PDF, the committee assumes the PDF was written by a consultant. Align them.

Rehearse a five-minute research oral: identity sentence, one finished paper with a result in plain language, one paper in flight with a data sentence, one ask (data access, doctoral students, seminar slot). Rehearse a three-minute teaching oral: two courses, one method, one artefact you can show on a laptop if asked. Time both. Nervous candidates double the length and lose the expert.

Prepare for the hostile but fair questions that statements invite:

  • “This journal is not on our list.” Have a one-line defence of quality and a next-target plan, not a speech about rankings.
  • “We need someone for Cost Accounting, not ESG.” Say yes or no. A long pivot is a no dressed as a yes.
  • “How will you publish with our teaching load?” Answer with a weekly hour budget, not with hope.
  • “Your co-authors are all from your Ph.D. lab.” Name one solo or new-collaboration piece in the pipeline.
Classroom twin for placement cells. The same five-minute discipline helps M.Com. and MBA students who must explain a dissertation to a recruiter. Identity, one result, one next step. Faculty who teach that muscle produce better first jobs and better future colleagues.

7. Retire the six paragraphs that kill Indian shortlists

These appear so often they have become filters.

  1. Childhood origin story. “I have always been curious about markets.” Cut. Start with the work.
  2. Unverifiable passion. “I am passionate about teaching.” Replace with a course and a method.
  3. Name-dropping without a paper. Listing Nobel lectures you attended is not a pipeline.
  4. Inflated administrative claims. “I led NAAC Criterion 3” when you collected files for a coordinator. Say what you actually did.
  5. Predatory outlet as prestige. If the only “international journal” is a pay-to-publish title, do not headline it. Put honest working papers ahead of a bad publication.
  6. Copy-paste diversity paragraph written for a US form. Indian campuses need a sentence on language, first-generation students, or gender in the classroom if that is real in your practice. They do not need a US DEI boilerplate that names no Indian statute or student fact.
Do not. Use a generative model to write the statement and then “add a few Indian examples.” Committees now hear the cadence. Use a model, if at all, to cut words after you have drafted from your own papers and syllabi. The facts must come from your file.

A last mechanical check: print the statement. Read it with a red pen as if you were the tired subject expert at 4 p.m. Circle every adjective that is not attached to a noun the campus can verify. Delete those adjectives. What remains is the document.

8. Field checklist and closing brief

Before you upload, tick these in order.

  • Advertisement phrases circled; two of them answered with evidence in the first page.
  • Identity sentence names field, method family, and site.
  • At most three completed pieces; status and outlet clear.
  • Two working papers with data source and a 24-month date.
  • Two courses named from the target department’s handbook.
  • One teaching method and one artefact (syllabus, rubric, or assignment).
  • One honest sentence on load and how publishing survives it.
  • No origin story, no predatory headline, no US boilerplate.
  • PDF searchable, named, page-numbered; oral version timed to five and three minutes.
  • CV, statement, and sample paper do not contradict each other on dates or titles.

A statement that survives an Indian shortlist is a courtesy to the committee and a contract with yourself. You are telling a mixed room what you will do with their timetable and their letterhead. Write that contract in nouns. Leave the memoir for the campus novel you will not have time to write in year one.

Early-faculty careers in India are decided in rooms that are more bureaucratic than romantic. That is not an insult to the vocation. It is the reason a scorable two-page statement beats a beautiful six-page essay. Prepare the file the way you would prepare an identification section: name the claim, show the design, state what would falsify it. Here the “claim” is that you can teach their courses and finish your next two papers. Design is the pipeline and the syllabus. Falsification is a year with neither. Write so that a fair committee can bet on the first outcome.
